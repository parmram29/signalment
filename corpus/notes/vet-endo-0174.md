## Central hypothyroidism pathophysiology

Central hypothyroidism results from insufficient TRH (hypothalamic) or TSH (pituitary) production, leading to decreased thyroid hormone synthesis. Unlike primary hypothyroidism, the thyroid gland remains histologically normal but functionally under-stimulated. Causes include pituitary neoplasia, trauma, or developmental anomalies.

## Diagnostic differentiation

Central hypothyroidism typically shows low-normal or low TSH concentrations compared to elevated TSH in primary disease. Baseline T4 and T3 concentrations may overlap with primary hypothyroidism. TRH stimulation testing may help differentiate pituitary from hypothalamic causes based on TSH response patterns.

## Clinical implications

Concurrent pituitary hormone deficiencies may occur, necessitating evaluation of other axes. Response to levothyroxine therapy is typically favorable, though monitoring TSH may be less useful for dose adjustment compared to primary disease. Lifelong supplementation is usually required.
