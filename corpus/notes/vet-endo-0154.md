## Clinical signs in horses

Equine hypothyroidism presents differently than in dogs. Weight gain, lethargy, and poor performance are common. Reproductive dysfunction in mares and stallions may occur. Dry, dull haircoat and delayed shedding may be observed. Myxedema is rare compared to dogs.

## Diagnostic challenges

Baseline T4 and T3 concentrations have limited utility due to marked physiological variation. Baseline TSH may be elevated but is not consistently reliable. TRH stimulation testing evaluates pituitary reserve but is logistically challenging in field settings.

## Differential considerations

Many signs overlap with other conditions including PPID, seasonal variations, malnutrition, and other systemic illnesses. Age, season, reproductive status, and concurrent illness all affect thyroid function parameters. Exercise training may transiently suppress thyroid function.
