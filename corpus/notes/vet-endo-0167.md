## Clinical presentation differences

Feline hyperadrenocorticism differs from canine presentation. Polyuria/polydipsia may be less prominent due to concurrent diabetes mellitus masking these signs. Muscle wasting, pot-bellied appearance, and hepatomegaly occur but may be attributed to concurrent conditions. Dermatological signs are typically milder than in dogs.

## Diagnostic challenges

Standard ACTH stimulation and low-dose dexamethasone suppression tests have variable sensitivity in cats. Endogenous ACTH measurement helps differentiate PDH from ADH. Advanced imaging is crucial for treatment planning but technically challenging in cats.

## Association with diabetes

Up to 25% of diabetic cats have concurrent hyperadrenocorticism. Cortisol-induced insulin resistance complicates diabetes management. Recognition of both conditions is essential for appropriate treatment planning and prognosis.
