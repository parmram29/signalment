## Congenital hypothyroidism in piglets

Congenital hypothyroidism in swine may result from thyroid dysgenesis, dyshormonogenesis, or maternal iodine deficiency. The condition affects growth and development significantly. Affected piglets show stunted growth, coarse haircoat, and mental dullness. Breeding programs may inadvertently select for genetic defects.

## Acquired causes and management

Iodine deficiency remains an important cause of acquired hypothyroidism in swine. Goitrogenic substances in feed can interfere with thyroid function. Treatment involves iodine supplementation and thyroid hormone replacement. Monitoring involves growth parameters and thyroid function tests.

## Production implications

Hypothyroidism significantly impacts growth efficiency and feed conversion. Affected animals may not reach market weight in standard timeframes. Early detection and treatment are important for animal welfare and economic considerations in commercial operations.
