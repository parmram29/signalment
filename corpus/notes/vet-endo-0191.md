## Post-surgical hypoparathyroidism

Removal of parathyroid tissue during thyroid surgery or other cervical procedures can cause iatrogenic hypoparathyroidism in horses. The condition may be temporary if parathyroid function recovers or permanent if sufficient parathyroid tissue is removed or damaged. Recognition and appropriate management are crucial for preventing complications.

## Clinical presentation and emergency management

Acute hypocalcemia causes muscle fasciculations, tetany, and potentially life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias. Subtle signs may include hypersensitivity, muscle stiffness, and decreased performance. Emergency treatment involves slow IV calcium administration with cardiac monitoring. Long-term management requires oral calcium and vitamin D supplementation.

## Monitoring and prognosis

Regular monitoring of serum calcium, phosphorus, and ionized calcium is essential for appropriate supplementation adjustment. The prognosis for post-surgical cases depends on the extent of parathyroid damage and owner compliance with long-term supplementation protocols. Dietary management includes appropriate calcium to phosphorus ratios.
