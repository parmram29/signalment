## Iatrogenic hyperthyroidism

Most canine hyperthyroidism results from excessive levothyroxine supplementation for hypothyroidism. Less commonly, functional thyroid carcinomas may cause true hyperthyroidism. Dietary sources of excess thyroid hormone (meat-based treats from hyperthyroid cats) represent another potential cause.

## Clinical presentation differences

Dogs tolerate hyperthyroidism better than cats, often showing minimal clinical signs despite elevated T4 concentrations. Signs may include restlessness, panting, weight loss despite normal or increased appetite, and cardiac effects including tachycardia and potentially atrial fibrillation.

## Diagnostic considerations

Differentiating true hyperthyroidism from iatrogenic causes requires careful medication history. TSH concentrations are typically suppressed in true hyperthyroidism but may remain normal with iatrogenic causes. Imaging may identify thyroid masses or assess for concurrent conditions.
