## Primary hyperparathyroidism

Parathyroid adenomas cause autonomous PTH secretion, leading to hypercalcemia through increased bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and intestinal calcium absorption via calcitriol. Single adenomas account for ~85% of cases.

## Secondary hyperparathyroidism

Chronic kidney disease causes decreased renal phosphate excretion and reduced calcitriol synthesis, leading to hypocalcemia and compensatory PTH elevation. Aluminum toxicity from phosphate binders can cause adynamic bone disease and relative hypoparathyroidism.

## Fibrous osteodystrophy

Chronic PTH excess causes generalized osteopenia and pathologic fractures. Brown tumors (osteoclastomas) may develop in severe cases. Dental radiographs may reveal characteristic changes before systemic signs appear.
