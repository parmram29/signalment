## Alpha cell tumor pathophysiology

Glucagonomas arise from pancreatic alpha cells and secrete excessive glucagon, causing hyperglucagonemia. These tumors are typically malignant with early metastatic potential to liver and lymph nodes. Clinical signs result from glucagon excess and associated amino acid depletion.

## Characteristic syndrome

Necrolytic migratory erythema represents the hallmark cutaneous manifestation, affecting footpads, periorificial areas, and pressure points. Hepatopathy develops with vacuolar changes and may progress to cirrhosis. Weight loss, glossitis, and cheilitis are common constitutional signs.

## Diagnostic approach

Elevated plasma glucagon concentrations support diagnosis, though sample handling is critical. Advanced imaging identifies pancreatic masses and metastatic disease. Histopathology confirms alpha cell origin with immunohistochemical staining for glucagon.
