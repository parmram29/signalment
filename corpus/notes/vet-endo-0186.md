## Secondary hyperparathyroidism in grazing animals

Sheep grazing on low-calcium pastures may develop secondary hyperparathyroidism, particularly during lactation when calcium demand is high. Grass tetany (hypomagnesemia) may compound the issue by affecting parathyroid function. This condition is more common in older ewes with decreased bone calcium reserves.

## Pathophysiology of grass tetany

Magnesium deficiency affects PTH release and action, impairing the normal homeostatic response to hypocalcemia. Neurological signs result from both hypocalcemia and hypomagnesemia. Lactating ewes are at increased risk due to high calcium requirements for milk production.

## Prevention and management

Supplementation with calcium and magnesium is essential during high-risk periods. Pasture management may include providing hay with higher mineral content during critical periods. Monitoring involves observation for clinical signs and potentially periodic blood mineral assessment in high-risk flocks.
