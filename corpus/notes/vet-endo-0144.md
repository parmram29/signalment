## Pituitary-dependent hyperadrenocorticism

Approximately 80-85% of cases result from pituitary adenomas (predominantly microadenomas) secreting excessive ACTH. This stimulates bilateral adrenal hyperplasia and cortisol overproduction. Cortisol-secreting macroadenomas account for 15-20% of cases.

## Cortisol excess consequences

Chronic hypercortisolemia induces hepatopathy with elevated ALP and ALT, muscle wasting, dermal atrophy with calcinosis cutis, immunosuppression, and hypertension. The polyuria/polydipsia results from cortisol-induced renal medullary washout and secondary hypernatremia.

## Diagnostic challenges

Endogenous ACTH levels differentiate PDH from ADH, but sample handling is critical. Stress leukogram changes (lymphopenia, eosinopenia, monocytopenia, neutrophilia) may be blunted by concurrent illness.
