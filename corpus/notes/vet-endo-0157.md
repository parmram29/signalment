## Primary hyperaldosteronism

Adrenal adenomas (Conn's syndrome) autonomously secrete aldosterone, causing hypertension and hypokalemia. The condition is increasingly recognized with improved diagnostics. Bilateral adrenal hyperplasia is rare in cats compared to humans.

## Secondary hyperaldosteronism

Heart failure, chronic kidney disease, and cirrhosis activate renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, leading to secondary hyperaldosteronism. The physiological response aims to maintain perfusion pressure but can become maladaptive.

## Clinical consequences

Mineralocorticoid excess causes sodium retention, potassium loss, and volume expansion. Hypertension frequently causes retinal detachment, cardiac changes, and progressive renal injury. Hypokalemia causes muscle weakness and may potentiate arrhythmias.
