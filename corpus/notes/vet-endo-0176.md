## Mechanism of steroid-induced diabetes

Progestagens (particularly megestrol acetate and medroxyprogesterone acetate) cause insulin resistance through glucocorticoid-like effects on glucose metabolism. They enhance gluconeogenesis, reduce peripheral glucose uptake, and may have direct effects on pancreatic beta-cells, reducing insulin secretion.

## Clinical presentation

Onset is typically rapid, occurring within days to weeks of initiating progestin therapy. Signs include polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, and weight gain. Concurrent administration with glucocorticoids potentiates the diabetogenic effect. Some dogs may have subclinical glucose intolerance that becomes apparent with progestin exposure.

## Management approach

Discontinuation of progestin therapy may resolve diabetes if caught early, though permanent diabetes may develop with prolonged exposure. Management includes insulin therapy and addressing the underlying condition for which progestin was prescribed. Alternative therapies should be considered when possible.
