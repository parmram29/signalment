## IGF-1 physiology in cats

IGF-1 is produced primarily in the liver under GH stimulation and mediates many of GH's growth-promoting effects. In acromegalic cats, chronically elevated GH drives IGF-1 production, making it a useful biomarker for GH excess. Unlike GH, IGF-1 has a longer half-life and less pulsatile release pattern.

## Diagnostic utility

IGF-1 concentrations are elevated in acromegalic cats and correlate with disease severity. Values >1000 ng/mL strongly suggest acromegaly in the appropriate clinical context. However, IGF-1 may be elevated in other conditions including diabetes mellitus and hyperthyroidism, requiring clinical correlation.

## Monitoring therapeutic response

Serial IGF-1 measurements help monitor response to treatment interventions. Successful treatment (medical or radiation therapy) results in declining IGF-1 concentrations over months. Complete normalization may not occur even with successful treatment due to permanent pituitary damage.
