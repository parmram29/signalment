## Clinical presentation of equine Addison's disease

Primary hypoadrenocorticism in horses is rare but can occur due to immune-mediated adrenal destruction or other causes of adrenal insufficiency. Horses may present with weight loss, weakness, poor performance, and recurrent colic. Unlike other species, the classic electrolyte abnormalities may be subtle or absent.

## Diagnostic approach

Baseline cortisol concentrations may be low, but ACTH stimulation testing is required for definitive diagnosis. Differentiating from other causes of poor performance or weight loss requires systematic approach. Monitoring involves clinical signs and potentially serial hormone assessments.

## Management considerations

Treatment involves glucocorticoid and potentially mineralocorticoid replacement. Dosing must be adjusted for stress and illness. Prognosis depends on owner compliance with long-term medication and monitoring requirements. Careful attention to medication timing around stressful events is important.
