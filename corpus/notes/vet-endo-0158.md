## Platelet-mediated potassium release

Horses have large platelets (thrombocytes) with high intracellular potassium concentrations. During clot formation, platelet aggregation and degranulation release significant amounts of potassium, artificially elevating serum compared to plasma values. True hyperkalemia must be differentiated from this artifact.

## Clinical significance

True hyperkalemia causes cardiac conduction abnormalities including bradycardia, atrioventricular block, and potentially asystole. Distinguishing artifact from pathological hyperkalemia is critical for appropriate treatment. Heparinized plasma samples provide accurate potassium measurements.

## Association with endocrine disorders

PPID and EMS horses may have altered platelet function, potentially affecting the degree of pseudohyperkalemia. Additionally, insulin administration for EMS may cause true hypokalemia, which can complicate interpretation of potassium values.
