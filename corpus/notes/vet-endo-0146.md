## Etiology and pathogenesis

Multifocal thyroid adenomatous hyperplasia is the underlying cause in >95% of cases, with bilateral involvement in ~70%. Rarely, functional thyroid carcinomas may be responsible. Environmental factors including PBDE flame retardants and canned food consumption may contribute.

## Metabolic consequences

Excessive T4/T3 cause upregulation of Na+/K+ ATPase, increasing metabolic rate and oxygen consumption. Cardiac effects include increased contractility and heart rate, potentially progressing to hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Concurrent diabetes mellitus occurs in ~25% of cases.

## Treatment considerations

Methimazole inhibits thyroid peroxidase, blocking hormone synthesis. Transdermal formulations reduce compliance issues. Radioactive iodine therapy provides definitive treatment. Surgical thyroidectomy remains an option but risks parathyroid damage. T3 suppression may temporarily normalize in some cats without treatment.
