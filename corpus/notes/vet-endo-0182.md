## Iatrogenic and natural causes

Hyperthyroidism in cattle is rare but may result from excessive thyroid hormone supplementation or consumption of feed contaminated with thyroid tissue. Some cattle may develop autonomous thyroid nodules, though this is less common than in other species. Dietary sources of excess thyroid hormone include offal from hyperthyroid animals.

## Clinical manifestations

Affected cattle show increased metabolic rate, weight loss despite increased appetite, and nervousness. Reproductive effects include irregular estrous cycles, reduced conception rates, and embryonic mortality. Cardiovascular effects include tachycardia and potentially cardiac arrhythmias. Milk production may be affected.

## Diagnostic approach

Elevated T3 and T4 concentrations confirm the diagnosis. TSH concentrations are typically suppressed. Differentiating from stress-induced changes or assay interference requires careful clinical correlation. Treatment involves identifying and eliminating the source of excess thyroid hormone.
