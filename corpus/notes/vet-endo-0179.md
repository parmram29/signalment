## Reactive hypercortisolemia mechanisms

Chronic illness, pain, stress, or medications can cause sustained HPA axis activation without pituitary or adrenal pathology. Conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, chronic infections, or cancer may result in persistent cortisol elevation that mimics true hyperadrenocorticism.

## Diagnostic differentiation

Differentiating pseudo-Cushing's from true Cushing's disease requires careful evaluation of diagnostic test results. The ACTH stimulation test and low-dose dexamethasone suppression test may show intermediate results. Urinary cortisol:creatinine ratios may be elevated but typically not as dramatically as in true Cushing's.

## Clinical management

Treatment focuses on addressing the underlying condition causing the HPA axis activation. Cortisol levels should normalize when the inciting cause is resolved. Monitoring response to treatment of the underlying condition helps confirm the diagnosis.
