## Catecholamine excess effects

Functional pheochromocytomas secrete epinephrine and norepinephrine, causing episodic hypertension, tachycardia, and systemic vasoconstriction. Alpha-1 receptor activation causes intense vasoconstriction, while beta-1 effects increase cardiac output. Clinical signs are often episodic and may mimic other conditions.

## Diagnostic approach

Plasma free metanephrines provide the most sensitive screening test. Clonidine suppression testing helps differentiate from stress-induced catecholamine elevation. Abdominal imaging identifies the mass and assesses for metastatic disease. Pre-surgical alpha-blockade with phenoxybenzamine is essential to prevent hypertensive crisis.

## Surgical considerations

Adrenalectomy carries high morbidity due to fragile adhesions and risk of intraoperative hypertensive crisis. Perioperative management includes continuous blood pressure monitoring and calcium channel blockers. Bilateral disease occurs in ~10% of cases, requiring staged surgery.
