## Type B insulin resistance pathophysiology

Type B insulin resistance occurs when autoantibodies bind to insulin receptors, blocking insulin binding and action. This rare condition causes severe insulin resistance in cats. The autoantibodies may have both blocking and stimulatory properties, occasionally causing hypoglycemia when bound insulin dissociates.

## Clinical presentation

Cats present with severe diabetes mellitus that is extremely difficult to regulate despite high insulin doses. Some cats may experience alternating episodes of hyperglycemia and hypoglycemia. Weight loss persists despite polyphagia. Concurrent autoimmune conditions may be present.

## Diagnostic approach

Testing for insulin autoantibodies confirms the diagnosis. High-dose insulin therapy is typically required. Immunosuppressive therapy with prednisolone or other agents may be beneficial in some cases, though this creates additional challenges for glucose control.
