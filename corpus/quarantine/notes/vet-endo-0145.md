## Primary adrenal insufficiency

Autoimmune adrenocortical destruction accounts for most cases, with anti-adrenal antibodies targeting enzymes in steroidogenic pathways. Bilateral destruction of >90% of adrenal cortex is required before clinical signs manifest. Mineralocorticoid deficiency predominates initially.

## Electrolyte abnormalities

Aldosterone deficiency causes sodium loss and potassium retention. The Na/K ratio <27:1 is pathognomonic. Hyperkalemia causes cardiac conduction abnormalities (bradycardia, widened QRS complexes, potentially asystole).

## Atypical presentations

Some patients retain mineralocorticoid function but lose glucocorticoid production, presenting with waxing/waning GI signs during stress without electrolyte abnormalities. These cases require ACTH stimulation testing for diagnosis.
