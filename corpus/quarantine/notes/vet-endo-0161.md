## Nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidism

Improper Ca:P ratios in captive reptiles lead to hypocalcemia and compensatory PTH elevation. Diets with excessive phosphorus or inadequate vitamin D3 prevent proper calcium absorption. This is particularly problematic in growing animals with high calcium demands.

## Metabolic bone disease

Chronically elevated PTH causes excessive bone resorption, leading to fibrous osteodystrophy. Bones become rubbery and prone to spontaneous fractures. Jaw involvement (rubber jaw) occurs in severe cases. Radiographic changes include generalized osteopenia and cortical thinning.

## Prevention strategies

Proper UVB lighting enables cutaneous vitamin D3 synthesis in diurnal species. Appropriate Ca:P ratios (2:1 to 1:1) with calcium supplementation when needed. Dietary variety helps ensure adequate nutrient intake. Husbandry requirements vary significantly between species.
