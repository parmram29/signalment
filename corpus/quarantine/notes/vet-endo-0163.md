## Glucocorticoid-deficient Addison's disease

Some cats lose glucocorticoid production while maintaining mineralocorticoid function, presenting with vague signs of GI upset, anorexia, and weakness during stress without electrolyte abnormalities. Diagnosis requires ACTH stimulation testing with cortisol measurement.

## Clinical recognition challenges

Atypical presentation lacks the classic electrolyte abnormalities of classical Addison's disease. Cats may present with chronic intermittent vomiting, weight loss, and poor appetite. Stress-induced decompensation may precipitate crisis with typical signs.

## Diagnostic approach

Baseline cortisol concentrations are unreliable. ACTH stimulation testing with cortisol measurement is required for diagnosis. Endogenous ACTH levels may be elevated but are not routinely measured. Differentiating from other causes of similar signs requires systematic approach.
