## Adrenal gland pathophysiology

Adrenocortical disease in ferrets involves hyperplasia, adenomas, or carcinomas of the adrenal cortex. Unlike dogs and cats, ferret adrenal lesions often produce sex hormones (estrogens, androgens) in addition to corticosteroids. The condition affects predominantly gonadectomized ferrets, possibly due to loss of negative feedback.

## Sex hormone effects

Excessive estrogen production causes bone marrow suppression, leading to anemia, thrombocytopenia, and leukopenia. Females may develop persistent estrus with vulvar swelling. Males may show prostate enlargement and associated urinary issues. Androgen excess causes aggressive behavior and increased musk production.

## Treatment considerations

Surgical adrenalectomy remains the preferred treatment, though medical management with deslorelin implants can provide temporary relief. Post-operative monitoring is important due to potential complications. Prognosis varies depending on the extent of bone marrow suppression and concurrent conditions.
