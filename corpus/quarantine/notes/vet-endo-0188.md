## Pathophysiology of medullary hyperplasia

Adrenal medullary hyperplasia may represent precursor lesions to pheochromocytomas in cats. The condition involves proliferation of chromaffin cells that secrete catecholamines. Unlike dogs and humans, pheochromocytomas are rare in cats, but medullary hyperplasia may be more common than previously recognized.

## Clinical manifestations

Cats with medullary hyperplasia may show episodic signs of catecholamine excess including hypertension, tachycardia, and behavioral changes. Signs may be subtle and intermittent, making diagnosis challenging. Hypertensive crises may occur during stress or handling.

## Diagnostic approach

Blood pressure monitoring and assessment of catecholamine metabolites (metanephrines) may be helpful. Advanced imaging can identify adrenal enlargement, though distinguishing hyperplasia from early neoplasia is difficult. Histopathology is required for definitive diagnosis.
