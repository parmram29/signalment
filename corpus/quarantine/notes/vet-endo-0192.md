## Central diabetes insipidus pathophysiology

Central diabetes insipidus in horses results from inadequate ADH production or release from the posterior pituitary. Causes include head trauma, pituitary neoplasia, or inflammatory conditions. The condition is rare in horses compared to other species but should be considered in cases of polyuria/polydipsia.

## Clinical presentation and diagnostic approach

Affected horses drink large volumes of water and produce dilute urine. Differentiation from other causes of polyuria/polydipsia requires water deprivation testing and potentially ADH response testing. Serum and urine osmolality measurements help confirm the diagnosis. Advanced imaging may identify structural abnormalities.

## Treatment considerations

Desmopressin acetate (DDAVP) may be used to manage central DI, though effectiveness varies. Management involves ensuring unlimited water access to prevent dehydration. Addressing underlying causes when possible is important for long-term management. Monitoring involves water intake and general health assessment.
