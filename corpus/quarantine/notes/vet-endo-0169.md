## Pathophysiology of estrogen excess

Chronic egg-laying, reproductive disorders, or ovarian neoplasia may cause sustained estrogen elevation. Excess estrogen affects hepatic protein synthesis, leading to hyperlipidemia and increased clotting factor production. Hepatic lipidosis may develop due to increased very-low-density lipoprotein synthesis.

## Clinical manifestations

Affected birds may develop hepatomegaly, coelomic distension, and dyspnea. Increased risk of thromboembolic events occurs due to elevated clotting factors. Polyuria may result from altered renal concentrating ability. Behavioral changes during reproductive cycles may be exaggerated.

## Management strategies

Environmental manipulation to reduce reproductive stimuli includes decreasing photoperiod, removing nesting materials, and separating bonded pairs. Medical intervention with GnRH agonists or antagonists may suppress estrogen production. Dietary management includes reducing calories and fat to prevent hepatic lipidosis.
