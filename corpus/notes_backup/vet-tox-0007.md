## Why cats are affected and dogs are not

Permethrin is a synthetic pyrethroid used in concentrated spot-on parasiticides
labelled for dogs. Cats are markedly more sensitive to it than dogs. Two factors
are usually given: cats have limited hepatic glucuronidation capacity, which
slows conjugation and clearance, and feline voltage-gated sodium channels are
comparatively sensitive to pyrethroid binding.

## Clinical picture

Prolonged sodium channel opening produces repetitive neuronal firing. Signs are
neuromuscular and typically appear within hours: tremors, twitching, ataxia,
hyperaesthesia, and in severe cases seizures and hyperthermia secondary to
sustained muscle activity.

## The exposure route that matters

Most feline cases are not deliberate. They follow a dog-labelled product being
applied to a cat, or a cat grooming a recently treated in-contact dog. This is
the canonical reason a veterinary retrieval system must filter by species
before ranking: the canine and feline documents for this drug are semantically
near-identical and clinically opposite.
